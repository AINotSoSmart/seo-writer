### Selecting reference photos for breed accuracy

Breed accuracy relies on capturing the specific anatomical markers that define a lineage. While basic filters might produce a generic animal, our engine requires distinct visual data to preserve your pet's individual character.

Modern editors use [harmonization](https://helpx.adobe.com/photoshop/desktop/repair-retouch/remove-objects-fill-space/blend-subjects-with-harmonize.html) to sync your pet's original lighting with the target scene. This process adjusts the color temperature and shadow depth of the reference photo to match the family portrait environment. Without it, the pet appears as a flat "cutout" rather than a three-dimensional part of the family.

Professional compositors often merge elements from several photos—using a body from one shot and a head from another—to overcome poor original imagery. You should follow this **multi-photo strategy** to ensure every breed-specific detail remains intact.

1. **Eye-Level Perspective**: Shooting from above compresses a pet's legs and distorts their frame. Capture them at their own eye level to keep their **skeletal proportions** true to the breed standard.
2. **Texture Clarity**: High-resolution shots allow the AI to map individual hairs. This is vital for breeds with complex coats, like Wire-haired Pointing Griffons, where **fur texture** defines the silhouette.
3. **Profile Geometry**: Provide a side-profile shot. This helps the tool understand the "stop" (the bridge between forehead and muzzle) and the neck length, which vary wildly between a Boxer and a Great Dane.
4. **Lighting Consistency**: Avoid direct flash, which flattens the face. Use **diffused daylight** to reveal the natural muscle contours and facial structure.
